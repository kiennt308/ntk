using multi-scale dynamic CNN and

Zhuoling Cheng1, Xuekui Bu1, Qingnan Wang2, Tao Yang3 & Jihui Tu1
Emotions play a crucial role in human thoughts, cognitive processes, and decision-making. EEG has 
become a widely utilized tool in emotion recognition due to its high temporal resolution, real-time 
monitoring capabilities, portability, and cost-effectiveness. In this paper, we propose a novel end-to-
end emotion recognition method from EEG signals, called MSDCGTNet, which is based on the Multi-
Scale Dynamic 1D CNN and the Gated Transformer. First, the Multi-Scale Dynamic CNN is used to 
extract complex spatial and spectral features from raw EEG signals, which not only avoids information 
loss but also reduces computational costs associated with the time-frequency conversion of signals. 
Then, the Gated Transformer Encoder is utilized to capture global dependencies of EEG signals. This 
encoder focuses on specific regions of the input sequence while reducing computational resources 
through parallel processing with the improved multi-head self-attention mechanisms. Third, the 
Temporal Convolution Network is used to extract temporal features from the EEG signals. Finally, the 
extracted abstract features are fed into a classification module for emotion recognition. The proposed 
method was evaluated on three publicly available datasets: DEAP , SEED, and SEED_IV. Experimental 
results demonstrate the high accuracy and efficiency of the proposed method for emotion recognition. 
This approach proves to be robust and suitable for various practical applications. By addressing 
challenges posed by existing methods, the proposed method provides a valuable and effective solution 
for the field of Brain-Computer Interface (BCI).
Keywords EEG signals, Temporal-spatial features, Multi-scale dynamic 1D CNN, Gated transformer 
encoder, Temporal convolution network
Emotions are physiological and cognitive reactions that humans experience in response to events or situations, 
which play a crucial role in human thoughts, cognitive processes, and decision making. Emotion analysis has 
found wide applications in various fields 1, including disease diagnosis, psychological analysis and evaluation, 
and social behavior analysis 2, etc. Traditional emotion analysis methods used non-physiological signals to 
detect the emotion state, including facial expression, speech, and behavioral characteristics 3–6. However, these 
methods are often influenced by individual and cultural differences, which leads to subjective interpretations 
of emotions and potential misidentification. With the rapid development of brain-computer interface (BCI)7–9, 
Electroencephalography (EEG)10 is a technique that captures brain signals corresponding to various states from 
the scalp surface area through the use of BCI. These signals are often categorized into different frequency bands, 
each associated with specific brain activities. These frequency bands are delta, theta, alpha, beta, and gamma, 
which covers a range from 0.1 Hz to more than 100 Hz. EEG has been extensively used in emotion research 
because of its high temporal resolution, real-time monitoring, portability, and cost-effectiveness. Therefore, the 
application of EEG to emotion analysis has garnered widespread attention and has become a research hotspot.
Many emotion recognition methods based on EEG signals have been proposed. The clustering method, as 
described in11,12, organizes EEG signals into clusters with similar characteristics based on the intrinsic structure 
of the data. While this method is known for its simplicity and efficiency, it is susceptible to producing incorrect 
detections. The primary challenges lie in the selection of initial centers and determining the appropriate number 
of clusters. Errors in these aspects can lead to suboptimal clustering results and compromise the accuracy of the 
detection process. Machine learning methods, as outlined in13–16, employ manual feature selection and classifiers 
for emotion detection, yielding improved performance compared to clustering methods. The key advantage lies

RNN extract temporal features. This approach tends to yield more satisfactory results compared to using CNN 
alone, but these methods require preprocessing of the data using time-frequency transformation, which could 
potentially lead to a loss of information and increase computational demands. The Transformer24 is an Encoder-
Decoder architecture with a Self-Attention Mechanism introduced by Vaswani et al. in 2017. Initially proposed 
for natural language25 processing tasks like machine translation, its versatility has led to widespread adoption in 
various domains, including computer vision, speech recognition, and more. The advantages of the transformer 
are attributed to its parallelization capabilities, scalability to handle long sequences, and its effectiveness in 
capturing contextual information. Consequently, the transformer framework has been increasingly applied to 
EEG emotion detection 26,27, which is achieving better performance than the combined method of CNN and 
LSTM.
The above methods have achieved satisfactory results, but the following challenges still need to be addressed: 
(1) Reduction of complexity. The currently existing methods require a pre-processing process, that is, time-
frequency transformation, which can be effective in extracting features from signals in both time and frequency 
domains. However, this approach poses the following problems: firstly, time-frequency conversion results in 
information loss and increased computational load; secondly, the preprocessing introduces complexity into the 
system. (2) Prediction accuracy and robustness. Given that EEG signals are the time sequence data, different 
segments of the sequence have varying levels of importance and influence on the prediction result. Existing 
models often overlook this issue, which can result in failure to capture crucial patterns or information present 
in the EEG data. This can ultimately lead to a reduction in both performance and generalization capabilities. 
In this paper, motivated by the practical application of EEG emotion detection, we propose a novel end-to-end 
emotion recognition method from EEG signals using a Multi-Scale Dynamic CNN and a Gated Transformer 
Encoder, named MSDCGTNet. Experiments were conducted on three public datasets: DEAP , SEED, and 
SEED_IV , achieving impressive accuracy results of 99.66%, 98.85%, and 99.67%, respectively. Our model not 
only provides novel and robust tools for EEG-based emotion recognition but also holds significant potential for 
diverse applications within brain-computer interface (BCI) systems.
In short, the novelty and significant contributions of the study are as follows:
 (1)  In this paper, we propose a novel end-to-end framework that utilizes the Multi-Scale Dynamic 1D CNN 
and the Gated Transformer Encoder for EEG-based emotion recognition. This unique framework addresses 
the challenges of EEG feature extraction and emotion classification as a unified process. Additionally, it 
reduces the model’s complexity.
 (2)  The Multi-Scale Dynamic 1D CNN is employed to extract spatial-spectral features from raw EEG signals, 
which not only helps avoid information loss caused by time-frequency conversion but also significantly 
enhances recognition performance.
 (3)  The Gated Transformer Encoder is used to extract global features from EEG signals. The gated layers can 
learn to dynamically select relevant features, which is particularly useful for EEG data where important 
signals may vary across time and channels. By incorporating non-linear transformations, the model can 
better capture complex relationships in the data that are not easily addressed by linear operations alone. 
Furthermore, the improved multi-head self-attention mechanism efficiently handles long EEG sequences 
compared to traditional Transformers, thereby reducing computational complexity and making it suitable 
for real-time processing of large-scale EEG signals.
 (4)  We compared our method with other state-of-the-art approaches using three public datasets: DEAP , SEED, 
and SEED_IV . The results demonstrate that our method achieves superior recognition accuracy and effi -
ciency compared to other leading methods.

Multi-scale dynamic 1D CNN
At present, most existing methods for EEG-based emotion recognition rely on time-frequency conversion 
since CNN network typically requires image-like inputs. This involves transforming EEG signal into various 
spectrograms, such as Fbank, MFCC, and speech spectrograms. From which multiple features are then extracted 
for recognition. However, these methods have several limitations: (1) Information Loss. Time-frequency 
conversion leads to information loss, thereby reducing the accuracy of recognition. (2) Computational Time. 
The time-frequency conversion process consumes significant computing resources, which can hinder real-time 
EEG processing. (3) Non-stationary and Dynamic. EEG signals are non-stationary and dynamic, and features 
extracted through serial convolution may be too simplistic, decreasing the model’s generalization ability. To 
address these limitations, we propose the Multi-Scale Dynamic 1D CNN, inspired by the pioneering work 
discussed in26. This method allows for efficient feature extraction directly from raw EEG signals, circumventing 
the drawbacks associated with time-frequency conversion.
As shown in Fig.  3, the multi-scale dynamic convolution module is designed to effectively capture the 
dynamic frequency characteristics of EEG signals by using convolution kernels of different sizes. The detailed 
steps are as follows: ① Temporal Convolution. Each convolution kernel applies a frequency filter for temporal 
convolution to capture variations in the time domain. ② Spatial Convolution. Convolution with a kernel size of 
(C, 1) is performed to process EEG signals in the spatial domain, focusing on the spatial relationships between 
EEG channels. ③ Dimensionality Reduction. An average pooling layer with a size of (1, 4) is used to reduce the 
dimensionality of the temporal features, and dropout technology is employed to prevent overfitting. ④ Feature 
Fusion. The concatenation layer fuses the output features from the pooling layer. As a result, the tensor obtained 
after the multi-scale dynamic convolutional module represents signal sources from different frequencies and 
electrode channels.

to a specific ratio of the EEG sampling frequency f. The αi determines the relationship between the convolution 
kernel size K and the EEG sampling frequency f, where i represents the scale level in the multi-scale network.

The gated transformer encoder
Each convolutional layer under the CNN framework shares weights, which means that they focus equally on each 
part of the EEG signal. To learn the global dependencies in EEG signals and focus on the relevant parts crucial 
for classification tasks, the Gated Transformer Encoder is proposed to capture global features in EEG signals, 
which are crucial for understanding the overall context and dependencies within the data. The multi-head self-
attention mechanism in the Transformer encoder allows the model to focus on different parts of the input data 
simultaneously, identifying the most relevant features for the classification task. The GLU layer combines linear 
transformations with a gating mechanism, which helps in selectively passing important features while filtering 
out less relevant information. This improves model performance and efficiency by reducing computational 
overhead.

The improved multi-head self-attention mechanism
Since Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) tend to lose a significant amount of information during long-distance 
propagation and are not very sensitive to feature importance, they can struggle to capture complex dependencies 
in data, which can lead to suboptimal performance in tasks that require understanding intricate relationships 
or maintaining long-range contextual information. This limitation often necessitates the use of more advanced 
architectures, such as Transformers, which utilize self-attention mechanisms to better preserve and emphasize 
critical features across long sequences. However, the computational complexity of the Multi-Head Self-
Attention mechanism is dominated by the dot-product calculation, which is O(n2·d). This quadratic dependence 
on the sequence length n makes self-attention computationally expensive for long sequences. To address the 
computational and memory challenges associated with self-attention, an improved attention mechanism 
method30 is proposed to reduces complexity by projecting the keys and values to a lower-dimensional space, 
which is achieve linear complexity relative to sequence length. This approach aims to retain the benefits of self-
attention while making it feasible to apply to longer sequences and larger datasets.

Temporal convolutional network (TCN)
After extracting EEG signal features of different frequencies and electrode channels, TCN module32 is applied to

is composed of stacked residual blocks, with each residual block consisting of two dilated causal convolution

normalization (Batch Norm) and an Exponential Linear Unit (ELU) activation function.

Experiment
In this section, experimental results are presented to verify the performance of our method. First, the experiment 
data, environment, evaluation metrics and hyperparameter setting are introduced in detail. Then, both 
the efficiency and the effectiveness of our method are evaluated and compared with several state-of-the-art 
methodologies. Finally, the ablation experiments and parameter analysis were employed to further evaluate our 
proposed approach.
Datasets
Three open-source datasets (DEAP , SEED, SEED_IV) are utilized as the test datasets to access the performance of 
the proposed method. These datasets used in our experimentation and the experimental protocol are described 
in detail as follows.
 (1)  The DEAP dataset33, created by the Queen Mary University of London, is designed for emotion analysis. It 
consists of physiological signals recorded from 32 participants while they watch 40 music video clips. All 
participants were students, consisting of 16 males and 16 females, with ages ranging from 19 to 37 years 
and an average age of 26.9 years. They were all in good physical health, had no mental disorders or brain 
injuries, and were right-handed. These stimuli are carefully selected to evoke various emotions, which pro-
vides a rich dataset for researchers to study the relationship between physiological responses, particularly 
EEG signals. 63 s of EEG signal data were collected by 32-channel Biosemi ActiveTwo device according to 
the international 10–20 system. The first 3 s of signals were the pre-trial baseline signals, and the remaining 
60 s of EEG recordings were mood signals. At the end of each experiment, the subjects performed a self-as-
sessment of their arousal and valence to determine whether the corresponding emotions were accurately 
elicited. The EEG signals were downsampled to 128 Hz. The electromyography (EMG) and electroencepha-
logram (EOG) signals were removed before the dataset was released. They were passed through a bandpass 
filter between 4 and 45 Hz to filter out noise. Emotions were classified into 4 categories based on the degree 
of arousal and level of valence (1–9) of each marker: happy, sad, fearful, and neutral.
 (2)  The SEED dataset34 is a large-scale open-source dataset developed by BCMI Labs for EEG-based emotion 
classification tasks, which can be divided into 3 categories of emotions (positive, neutral, and negative). 
Emotion clips were meticulously selected, ensuring that the clips can reliably evoke the corresponding emo-
tions in subjects. The dataset consists of 15 emotion clips extracted from different Chinese films, each clip 
is approximately 4 min long and exclusively depicts one type of emotion. Fifteen healthy native Chinese 
students, including seven males and eight females, with an average age of 23, participated in the EEG signal 
acquisition experiment. EEG signals were recorded using a 62-channel ESI NeuroScan system with a sam-
ple rate of 1000 Hz. An experiment consisted of fifteen trials with each trial corresponding to an emotion 
film clip. Before each clip, there was a 5-s hint, followed by a 45-s self-assessment period, and a 15-s rest 
after each clip. After the experiment, based on the subjects’ responses, only the sessions in which the target 
emotion was successfully elicited were selected for further analysis. The experiment was repeated three 
times for each subject. After the three sessions, all sessions for each subject were combined. EEG signals 
heavily contaminated by EMG and EOG were manually removed before the dataset was released. For data 
processing, the original EEG signals were first down-sampled to 200 Hz and then filtered to 4–50 Hz.
 (3)  The SEED-IV dataset35 is an evolution of the original SEED dataset, which is a multimodal dataset that in-
clude 62-channel EEG signals from 15 subjects, including eight females. Each subject watch 24 movie clips 
with a duration of approximately 2 min, introducing a change in the number of emotion categories from 
three to four: happy, sad, fear, and neutral. In addition to EEG signals, SEED-IV provides eye movement 
features recorded by SMI eye-tracking glasses. This multimodal dataset enhances its utility for emotion rec-
ognition by incorporating both EEG and eye movement data. For data processing, the original EEG signals 
were first down-sampled to 200 Hz and then filtered to 4–50 Hz.
Experimental settings
All experiments were conducted on the Dell 5810 server with the following main parameters: Intel Xeon E5-2660 
processor, 64G of RAM, NVIDIA 2080Ti graphics card, and Centos 7.0 operating system. The Adam algorithm 
is used for training, the cross-entropy is employed as the loss function, and the entire model and recognition 
process were implemented using PyTorch framework.
For training the model, Adam was used with a batch size of 20 for all three datasets. The configuration for 
the learning rate, number of epochs, and weight decay varied for each dataset. DEAP dataset: Learning rate of 
1e−5, 300 epochs, and weight decay of 0.01. SEED and SEED_IV datasets: Learning rate of 1e −4, 200 epochs, 
and weight decay of 1e−4.

Accuracy analysis
Accuracy (ACC) and Standard Deviation (STD) were used to evaluate the accuracy of emotion recognition 
and compare the performance of our method against existing approaches. The experiment is divided into two 
segments: (1) Assessment of Various Feature Selections with Transformer Encoder. This sub-section involves

the impact of different features on the performance of the method. (2) Comparison with Existing Methods. The 
second sub-section focuses on comparing the performance of the proposed method with existing approaches. 
This step aims to provide insights into how well the proposed method performs relative to established methods 
in the field. In addition, a t-test is a statistical hypothesis test used to determine if there is a significant difference 
between the means of two groups. It is a simple yet powerful tool for comparing the means of two methods. By 
selecting the appropriate test type, calculating the t-statistic, and interpreting the p-value, we can objectively 
evaluate whether the observed differences between methods are significant. In our experiment, the p-value 
between the proposed MSDCGTNet and other methods was computed to assess whether the observed 
differences are due to chance or represent a true distinction between the two approaches.

using EEG signal rely on time-frequency conversion. This involves transforming the EEG signal into various 
spectrograms, such as wavelets 36–38, spectrograms and FFT (Fourier transform). Subsequently, multiple 
features are extracted from these spectrograms and fed into transformer encoder for emotion recognition.

Interpretability of the model
SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) is a method for interpreting complex deep learning models by assigning 
an importance value to each feature for a given prediction. These values help identify the contribution of 
individual features to the model’s predictions. Figure 10 illustrates the significance of the top 20 most influential 
features (channels) to the model across the three datasets. The figure highlights that the regions contributing to 
emotion detection include FP1, FP2, F3, F4, F7, and F8. These regions belong to the frontal area of the brain, 
which is responsible for cognitive and emotional regulation 47,48. This alignment indicates that the proposed 
emotion detection model corresponds well with the brain’s known working mechanisms, further supporting the 
model’s validity and effectiveness.
In summary, the experimental results demonstrate that our proposed method achieves high accuracy and 
efficiency in emotion recognition. Specifically, the method delivers satisfactory results across different datasets, 
indicating better adaptability to various EEG acquisition devices. However, our work has two limitations: (1) The 
model’s training and testing are conducted on a server, which limits its practicality for real-world applications. 
(2) The model relies on large training datasets and faces challenges in generalizing to data from different EEG 
devices, potentially hindering its applicability in scenarios with limited data availability. To address these issues, 
future work will explore strategies such as transfer learning49, domain adaptation, and model lightweighting to 
enhance the method’s applicability and efficiency.
Conclusion
In this paper, a novel End-to-End Emotion Recognition Method from EEG Signals Using Multi-Scale Dynamic 
CNN and Gated Transformer Encoder is proposed. First, Multi-Scale Dynamic CNN is used to extract the 
temporal-spatial features from raw EEG signals, which mitigates the information loss caused by time-frequency 
conversion but also greatly enhances the detection performance. Secondly, the gated transformer encoder is 
utilized to capture global dependencies of EEG signals, thereby improving prediction and generalization 
capabilities and reduces computational demands through parallel processing of multiple self-attention 
mechanisms. Then, the temporal convolution network is used to extract temporal features available for EEG 
signals. Finally, SoftMax is utilized for emotion state classification. The experimental results show satisfactory 
performance on three public datasets (DEAP , SEED, SEED_IV). The proposed approach holds great potential 
for diverse applications within brain-computer interface (BCI) systems. In the future work, we will focus on 
utilizing the proposed model for in-depth EEG signal analysis and its application in disease diagnosis and 
mental health detection.
