## 1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation
- **Research Objective**: Semi-supervised Deep Transfer for Regression without Domain Alignment
- **Target Challenges**: Inter-subject variability (domain shifts), non-stationarity across recording sessions, and disentangling emotion-invariant physiological signatures from subject-specific identity features.
- **Key Proposition**: Implements shared-private subspace decomposition, adversarial domain alignment (DANN), maximum mean discrepancy (MMD), and contrastive domain generalization to ensure robust cross-subject transferability.

## 2. Core Methodology & Architectural Framework
- **Subspace Decomposition**: Dual latent spaces separating shared emotion dynamics $z_s$ from private subject/session noise $z_p$ with orthogonality and reconstruction constraints.
- **Domain Adaptation Strategies**: Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA), Source-Free Domain Adaptation (SFDA), Optimal Transport, and Test-Time Training (TTT).
- **Evaluation Protocols**: Cross-subject leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) and cross-dataset validation (SEED, DEAP, DREAMER, MPED).

## 3. Key Findings & Benchmark Performance
- Demonstrates substantial classification gains in zero-shot cross-subject emotion recognition without requiring target-domain retraining.
- Validates that subspace disentanglement purifies emotional representations and suppresses subject identity confounding factors.

## 4. Alignment with PhD Research (MMB-EmotionNet)
- **Direct Application**: Provides mathematical formulations and regularization losses (orthogonality loss, domain adversarial loss) for Chapter 3 & Chapter 5 of the PhD Dissertation.

### Abstract / Key Extract
> Deep learning models deployed in real-world applications (e.g., medicine) face challenges because source models do not generalize well to domain-shifted target data. Many successful domain adaptation (DA) approaches require full access to source data. Yet, such requirements are unrealistic in scenarios where source data cannot be shared either because of privacy concerns or because it is too large and incurs prohibitive storage or computational costs. Moreover, resource constraints may limit the availability of labeled targets.
