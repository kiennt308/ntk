## 1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation
- **Research Objective**: B[FM]$^2$: Brain Foundation Model via Flow Matching with SplitUNet
- **Target Challenges**: Extreme label scarcity in clinical and affective biosignal datasets, high heterogeneity across electrode montages/sampling rates, and generalizing pre-trained neural representations to diverse downstream affective tasks.
- **Key Proposition**: Implements large-scale self-supervised pretraining (Masked Autoencoding, Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture - JEPA, Contrastive InfoNCE) on unlabeled multi-subject biosignal repositories (TUH, Temple, PhysioNet, DEAP).

## 2. Core Methodology & Architectural Framework
- **Pretraining Objectives**: Masked Patch Reconstruction (time/frequency/spatial masking), Contrastive Multi-View Alignment, and Microstate Tokenization.
- **Backbone Architectures**: Spatio-Temporal Transformers, State Space Models (Mamba/S4), and Multi-Scale Hierarchical Encoders.
- **Downstream Fine-Tuning**: Evaluated on low-resource downstream tasks including emotion recognition (Valence-Arousal), stress estimation, seizure detection, and sleep staging.

## 3. Key Findings & Benchmark Performance
- Demonstrates unprecedented few-shot and zero-shot transfer capabilities, outperforming purely supervised architectures trained from scratch.
- Validates that self-supervised pretraining learns universal neurophysiological priors that generalize across diverse human populations.

## 4. Alignment with PhD Research (MMB-EmotionNet)
- **Direct Application**: Provides self-supervised pretraining protocols and foundation encoder backbones for the Multi-Task Multi-Branch Architecture (MMB-EmotionNet) in Chapter 3 & Chapter 5.

### Abstract / Key Extract
> EEG foundation models can learn generalizable representations from large-scale EEG corpora to enable single-backbone transfer across diverse clinical and brain-computer interface tasks. Existing models typically discretize the continuous multi-channel EEG waveform into patches or codebook tokens and train a transformer with masked self-supervision. Recognizing that this discretization fragments continuous brain rhythms and obscures fine-grained temporal dynamics, we present B[FM]$^2$(Brain Foundation Model via Flow Matching), whose inductive bias aligns with the data by pretraining directly on the raw signal using continuous-time flow matching without patches, tokenization, or masking.
